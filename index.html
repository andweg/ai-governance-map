Frontier AI Governance Map

As of 11 August 2026. Not anywhere near complete.

Company self-governance

Actor / InstrumentGovernsWhat it doesTeeth?
Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy Anthropic Sets danger tiers ("ASL levels"). When a model reaches a tier, tougher security and deployment requirements kick in Weak, voluntary. The company holds itself to it, watched by its board and trust. It only becomes enforceable where a law (California's for example) requires publishing such a framework
OpenAI Preparedness Framework OpenAI Tracks specific dangerous capabilities and sets "High" and "Critical" thresholds that trigger safeguards before a model ships Weak, voluntary: a safety committee sitting at the nonprofit Foundation level can demand fixes or block a release
Google DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework Google DeepMind Defines "Critical Capability Levels" for bio and chemical weapons, cyberattacks, manipulation, and AI that speeds up AI research, each with a response plan Weak, voluntary. Reviewed by internal committees only
Meta, xAI and Microsoft frontier frameworks Each firm Similar danger-threshold structures as above None, voluntary. Internal only

Shared voluntary promises

Actor / InstrumentGovernsWhat it doesTeeth?
EU GPAI Code of Practice Companies offering general-purpose AI models in the EU Sign-up commitments: document your model, assess risks, report serious incidents, and have a copyright policy Moderate. Voluntary to sign, but signing is the practical way to show you comply with the EU AI Act. No penalty for breaking the code but the regulators monitor signatories
Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments ~20 frontier developers across the US, EU, UK, China and UAE Each promises to publish a safety framework with danger thresholds and lines it will not cross Weak, voluntary.
Frontier Model Forum Member labs (Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Amazon, Meta) Publishes shared technical guidance on danger thresholds, outside testing and security. Source of the field's common vocabulary None, voluntary.

Testing and certification

Actor / InstrumentGovernsWhat it doesTeeth?
EN ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems) Any organisation that wants the certificate Certifies that a company has AI management processes in place. Nothing about model safety, though Weak, voluntary. The certificate can be withdrawn, and it does not yet earn automatic credit towards EU AI Act compliance
CAISI (US Center for AI Standards and Innovation) Volunteering developers Tests models before release (30-day pre-release review), with a focus on cybersecurity, and works on standards None, voluntary. It can test a model but cannot approve or block one. Its findings are not published anymore
UK AI Security Institute Volunteering developers Tests models before release and does the underlying research None, voluntary. No power to approve or block the release
Independent evaluators — METR, Apollo Research, FAR.AI Whoever hires them Various evals on whether models can act on their own across long tasks, whether they deceive their operators, and whether they can meaningfully uplift a bad actor Weak, contractual. They publish their findings but are limited by confidentiality agreements and require the lab's permission to get access in the first place

Government bodies

Actor / InstrumentGovernsWhat it doesTeeth?
European AI Office Providers of general-purpose AI models The single EU regulator for these models: can demand documents, run its own tests, order fixes, restrict a model, and fine Strong. It's the law. Fines up to 3% of worldwide revenue or €15M, and the power to pull a model from the EU market. Limited in practice by how few technical staff it has
Department for Science, Innovation and Technology / UK AI Security Institute The UK Model testing (see above), risk assessment None directly. Sector regulators have real powers
US federal: CAISI, executive offices, AI Litigation Task Force US Sets rules for government procurement, tests models voluntarily (see above), and sues states over their AI laws Strong against states, none against developers. The Justice Department can take states to court and federal purchasing rules bind suppliers, but no federal body can tell a lab how to build a model
Cyberspace Administration of China Companies offering AI in China Requires registration and a security review before launch, review of training data, and labelling of AI-generated content Strong. It's the law. Can suspend a service or bar it from the market entirely
